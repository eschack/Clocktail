# Stability: the judge, the margin, the crash
What nvgwls is and how to run it, the lab beside it and which judge tests what, why games need 30-60 MHz more margin, what a crash looks like, and what to do after one.

## nvgwls.exe, NVIDIA's own stressor
- Ships with the driver (inside the driver store's NvContainer folder); NVIDIA App's auto-tune runs it. It only adds load; it never writes to the GPU.
- Work: graphics (tessellation, Blinn-Phong, texturing, full-screen depth overdraw, mesh shaders, integer ALU), compute (all SMs in lockstep, FP16/32/64) and ray tracing (procedural spheres, triangles, shadow rays).
- Judging: it renders once as the reference; every later result must match bit for bit (float images within 1e-6). It catches silent errors a game never shows.
- Exit codes: 0 pass, 1 cancelled, 2 runtime error, 3 TDR, 4 crashed, 5 RC/Xid, 6 data error (silent corruption). Not exited 30 s after its run time: hung (killed). A hang the driver recovers from also ends in code 2: `stress_test` reads the System log for a driver reset during the run (nvlddmkm 153, Display 4101) and calls that a hang, whatever the code (`driver_resets` in its answer).
- `stress_test load=100`: continuous full load. On the reference card it pins the 600 W wall within 1 s (~2880-2917 MHz on the tuned curve, 930-945 mV). A good fast judge: it reproduced a bad curve's hang within 30 s.
- `load` under 100: a 250 Hz square wave at that duty. `load=50` ran ~385 W at ~3055 MHz, the curve's top, as NVML reads it. NVML averages the gaps: timed from inside the GPU, the SMs ran 2480-2580 MHz within every burst, at every duty and every voltage from 900 to 1095 mV (the limiter acts within the burst). So a pulsed pass shows the top holds a light load's spikes, not that the top's points ran; `lab_test phases=solo` runs them (below).
- Combine with `set_power_limit` / `set_current_limit` to pin it lower down the curve.

## The lab (`lab_test`), Clocktail's own judge
- One engine (`clocktail-lab`, StabilityLab's, moved in), a process of its own, so a GPU that hangs takes the load down and not the agent: CUDA kernels the driver compiles, no toolkit. Every result is checked exactly: the compute against the CPU bit for bit (a chunk must equal the CPU-checked reference chunk byte for byte), every memory word, the soloist's blocks against each other. It reads the card through NVML and writes nothing; a job's trials go through the service.
- Its instruments, by what they judge:

| Family | Phase | What it plays | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core | `burn` | every thread on an FMA chain; `targetW` steers it to a board power | the wall: steady power, the middle of the curve |
| Core | `light` | one thread in `lightDiv`, few SMs busy | the climb to the top (2910 MHz at ~410 W at stock) |
| Core | `pulse` | one short full launch per game-like frame, 30-120 fps | the load step a game makes |
| Core | `solo` | the integer soloist, judged per pipe, divisi under the wall | the top of the curve, each point run for real |
| Core | `leap` | the soloist on the whole chip, then on a quarter of it, over and over | the soloist's pipes through the jump onto the top a game makes when a heavy scene turns light |
| Memory | `memory` | fill and verify the whole buffer in five access orders | every word, and the bandwidth per order (ST02) |
| Memory | `latency` | a pointer chase | ns per DRAM round trip (~500 ns at stock) |
| Uncore | `l2` | a 32 MiB region, a third of L2, over and over | the crossbar, the path XBAR clocks |

- **The soloist (`solo`, 0.4.2, from the Etude project).** The SMs that fail first on the reference card fail in the integer multiply-accumulate paths, the INT8 tensor core first, then the 64-bit integer MAC and dp4a, before plain fp32. The soloist plays those (weights fp32 1, int64 MAC 2, dp4a 2, INT8 mma 4) over a quiet fp32 line, each pipe in a lane of its own. Every block computes the same work, so per lane the blocks vote: one that differs computed it wrong, and the failure names its SM and pipe (`SM 44 mma8`). No CPU twin is needed, so the work can be anything the GPU has.
- **Divisi: a share of the SMs, so the clock stays on the curve.** The whole chip at the top draws the board onto its wall (600 W on the reference card), and there the clock is the limiter's, not the curve's. The soloist plays a window of the SMs that moves on by its own width every chunk, so every SM plays in turn; the share starts at all of them and is cut, 3 s at a time, until the board draws under 95 % of its enforced limit (aiming at 85 %; a board pinned to its limit reads the wall, not the load, so that cut goes a step further), then held, and cut again if the card warms onto the wall. The hold is the stretch since the last cut: 20 s is enough, 60 s is better.
- **Held: did the point really run?** Each block times its own work (cycles over GPU nanoseconds); the median SM's clock must be within 7 % of what NVML reads, every SM meant to play must have run its share in 95 % of the chunks, and the board must be off the wall. Otherwise the run is inconclusive, not a pass: nothing ran that point. The answer's phase says `held`, `eff_mhz` (the SMs' own clock), `solo_breadth` / `solo_sms`, `fill`, `limit_w` and, on a failure, `lane_mismatches` by SM and pipe.
- **The leap (`leap`, 0.4.3).** The soloist on the whole chip for 1.5 s, then on a quarter of it for 1.5 s, over and over. The whole chip holds the board on its wall and the clock in the middle of the curve; the quarter lets go, and the driver lifts the clock to the curve's top within a quarter of a second, the voltage following. Judged per pipe like the soloist; a hang is its failure (the GPU fault it leaves). Each cycle's heavy half (once settled) and light half (its peak) say whether the clock leapt: under 90 MHz of leap in half the cycles, the run is inconclusive (`held` false), since nothing made the move. The answer's phase says `leaps`, `leapt`, `heavy_mhz`, `peak_mhz` and `eff_mhz` (the SMs' own clock in the light halves).
- Why it exists: on 2026-10-27 the reference card ran a curve every steady test had passed (Etude's soloist edges, `solo` held at the top) and hung six times in two games (The Last of Us Part II, Gears of War). `live_start` caught five of them: each time a heavy scene turned light, the clock went from ~2850 MHz at ~925 mV to the curve's top within one 250 ms sample and froze within a second (100 % busy, power falling to ~110-125 W, ~30 s before the driver reset). Lowering the flat top from 3127 to 2947 MHz and XBAR from +560 to +400 did not stop it: it froze at whatever the top was. The person's older curve, ~2 MHz per mV above 885 mV, held. A steady test parks the clock on its point and never makes the move; the leap does nothing else.
- **What it does not catch.** On that same curve (待验证, flat 3127 MHz from 995 mV), `leap` made the games' move 80 times in two 120 s runs: at 500 W from 2835 MHz at 910 mV to 3120 MHz, the first sample still at 930 mV (the clock ahead of the voltage, as in the games), then 1005 mV, MSVDD 1060, XBAR 2902, ~260 W; at 600 W from 2962 MHz at 955 mV. No hang and no wrong lane. The jump alone does not hang this card: the games failed in work the soloist does not play (graphics, most likely: raster, texture, ROP, ray tracing). A `leap` pass is the soloist's pipes through the jump, not a game's; the top's last judge is still the person's games, with `live_start`.
- Reference card, the older curve "配置一" (870 mV up: 2692 MHz, 2902 at 935 mV, 3082 from 1040 mV), 60 s: 20 cycles, all leapt, the heavy halves at 2917 MHz and the light halves' peak at 3052 (the SMs' own clock 3049); pass.
- Reference card, the curve of 2026-10-27 morning (flat 3127 MHz from 995 mV, less the temperature's steps; the one that hung in the games), `solo` 60 s: the whole chip pinned the 600 W wall, 70 % of the SMs (119) drew 509 W and held there; the SMs ran 3088 MHz against NVML's 3082, 0 wrong lanes (20 s: the same share, 3095 against 3090). One bit flipped on purpose (`--inject-at`) failed it as `SM 44 mma8 x1`.
- **Which judge for what.** nvgwls covers raster, ray tracing and its own golden images, at full load (the wall: the middle of the curve) and pulsed; it maps the knee and the middle. The lab covers exact arithmetic, every memory word, chosen load shapes and speed: `solo` judges the top's points, where nvgwls's pulses never hold one, and `leap` the soloist's pipes through the jump onto them that games make; together they are the lab's last check of a curve before `keep_as_daily`, and the games come after; `memory`/`latency` and `l2` judge the memory and the uncore, and `tune_memory` / `tune_uncore` are made of them. Burn reached ~515 W and ~62 TFLOP/s at stock on the reference card. Neither replaces the person's games.
- A failure while a trial is in force (a bad bit, a dead context, a driver reset) is that trial's crash, as with `stress_test`. A run that could not be made is not: the lab stopping itself at the temperature limit (85 C by default, `maxTempC`; the jobs' measurements too) or when NVML gives no temperature for 10 s, a hang with no driver reset (a GPU hang brings one within the TDR delay), the lab crashing or writing nothing. Those are inconclusive: nothing is recorded against the setting, and a job stops instead of blaming it. Other work sharing the GPU (a local AI model, a game) is reported: bits are judged all the same, speeds are not the card's alone.
- While a run measures (`stress_test`, `lab_test`, each step of `tune_memory`, each comparison of `tune_uncore`) it holds judge.lock: every other write waits ("a stability run is in progress"), another agent's and the service's own (all but the fan guard giving the fans back to the curve at 85 C), so a result belongs to the setting it was measured on; the job's own next write carries its lease and goes through. A run starts only once no write is landing (the service answers "quiet"), and its crash goes to the trial that was in force when it began, never to one that took its place. A job also checks that the trial on the card is still the one it wrote when each run ends. Since 0.3.7 `stress_test` holds the card too: before, another agent could change it in the middle of a run. Two runs never overlap: the second is refused ("another run holds the card").

## Why games need more margin than the stressor
Measured, not assumed:
- Moving the curve down in 15 MHz steps from the nvgwls boundary: "boundary -15" crashed a game (550 W, 915-930 mV, after about a minute); "boundary -30" held.
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II crashed at 970 mV / 2940 MHz / 460 W / 73 C, where a 55 % duty nvgwls load had passed 2977 MHz at the same voltage, temperature and power for 150 s: the game was at least 37 MHz stricter. A 73-minute, 60-run nvgwls torture of that profile did not predict it.
- On the kept curve, games' p90 clock sat 39-74 MHz below the crash line (2940 + 2 x (mV - 967) MHz); 3DMark Speed Way 40-60 MHz.
- Crash points line up: game crashes and nvgwls full-load hangs fall on about 2 MHz per mV.

Hence the rule: **game-stable = the highest stress-passing clock minus 30-60 MHz**. A stress pass is necessary, not sufficient. The person playing their own games is the final test.

## Stability boundaries on the reference card (nvgwls, for orientation)
Full load, 60 s per step (passed / hung, MHz): 855 mV 2415 / -; 870-875 mV 2745 / -; 885 mV 2820 / 2820 (one run each way); 890 mV 2827 / 2835-2842; 895 mV 2842 / 2857; 910 mV 2880 / -; 915 mV - / 2887 (once). Slope ~16-20 MHz/mV at 855-875 mV, ~3-5 at 875-885, ~2-3 above: the curve's bend is 875-885 mV.
Light load (40 % duty, ~65 C): 930 mV 2947 / 2970; 940 mV 2970 / 3000; 955 mV 3015 / 3045; 975-985 mV 3105 / 3127; 1000-1005 mV 3097 / 3120. Light load is about 55 MHz looser than heavy load at 930 mV. These light-load points were pinned with `nvidia-smi -lgc` clock locks (`set_clock_lock` minMhz/maxMhz sets the same record); without one, a load lands where its power puts it on the curve.
Which nvgwls load reaches which voltage on the founders curve at 600 W: load 30 ~950 mV; 40-45 975-990 mV; 50 1005-1020 mV; 55-64 965-985 mV at 430-550 W; 72 and up the wall, 905-930 mV. Kingdom Come: Deliverance II crashed in the 970 mV middle band, which load 55-64 reaches: use it for that band.

## What a crash looks like
- **V/F hang (the usual one)**: the screen freezes for about 30 s (the TDR delay), the readings freeze at high clocks (3105-3180 MHz at 995-1090 mV, power falling to 125-200 W, utilization 100 %), then the driver resets (event `nvlddmkm 153`, Display 4101). nvgwls exits with code 2. No blue screen. The service records the trial as a crash and puts the daily profile back by itself.
- **Game crash to desktop or freeze, no driver reset**: the service cannot see it. Call `mark_crash` with what the person saw.
- **Whole-system crash** (bug check, reboot): rare; once from a 250 mV whole-curve left shift. The service checks for an unclean shutdown after boot.
- **Artefacts, flickering, odd colors**: treat as a crash: `back_to_daily`, then `mark_crash`.

## After a crash
1. A driver reset: the service has recorded the crash and puts the daily profile back once the GPU is idle (about 15 s; card_report says when it is on). A crash to the desktop with no reset: `mark_crash` first, which records it and puts the daily profile back. Then tell the person their card is safe.
2. `data_points`: which trial, which points, how long it ran.
3. The crash voltage the flight recorder reports is its pick of the last full-load sample before the freeze: it skips the frozen tail (with a 30 s TDR delay, ~30 s of the same clock and voltage at ~125 W) and the one sample of the limit letting go (power down, clock and voltage up), so on the reference hang it gives 2895 MHz at 910 mV / 598 W, not 2947 at 970. `data_points` puts the crash on the point nearest it. Check it when you can: If a live watch was running, its CSV has every sample (4 a second): find that last one there (`live_read` with tailSeconds shows the last 30 s). Otherwise use the trial's operating point.
4. Step that region down 15-30 MHz below the crash value; never retry the same value.
5. Note it with `log_note`, including the game and scene.

## Durations
A 60 s `stress_test` at load 100 catches a bad curve fast; 120-300 s before keeping something. Games: at least 5-10 minutes in the heaviest scene they know, per change.

## Guards that hold by themselves
- `stress_test` stops at 85 C (`maxTempC`, at most 88): a stop is neither a pass nor a crash. Cool the card (fans, case air) and run again.
- No trial is written while the GPU looks hung (frozen readings, reads that block): the write is refused (`gpu_hung`) and the driver reset that usually follows is recorded by the service.
- A failed run reports what it ran at before the failure, not the frozen readings after it.
- `begin_session unattended=true` for a mapping run nobody watches: after `maxCrashes` crashes (2 by default, 1..10) no new trial is written until a new session (`crash_budget`), so the card is not hung over and over with nobody there. The daily profile stays on.

## Windows' timeout (TDR)
card_report's "Driver timeout (TDR)" row reads Windows' own settings, never writes them. With TdrLevel 3 (the default) a hung GPU is reset after TdrDelay seconds (2 by default, 30 on the reference machine); until then the readings freeze at the curve's top, which is why a crash's frozen tail lasts that long. stress_test waits TdrDelay + 15 s (at least 30) past its run time before it calls a run hung. With TdrLevel 0 or 1 a hang does not recover by itself (the PC freezes, or bug-checks): do not map the curve's edge until it is 3.
